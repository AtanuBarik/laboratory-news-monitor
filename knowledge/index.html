ARUP says it is the first large U.S. reference laboratory to offer spectral flow cytometry for clinical use. Unlike conventional flow cytometry, spectral flow captures the full light spectrum and can evaluate many fluorescent markers simultaneously. ARUP's multiple-myeloma assay uses 18 markers in a single-tube design, reducing specimen-volume requirements while enabling deep characterization of very small neoplastic cell populations in bone marrow. MRD assessment is clinically important because the quantity of residual malignant cells after treatment helps physicians evaluate response, prognosis and relapse risk.
